# Where the frame goes: a profile at Phase 4 (2026-10-03)

The groundwork for Phase 6 of the lighting plan
(`docs/research/lighting-fidelity.md`, section 4): one profiling pass over
the frame as it stands at commit `50b3be8` (Phase 4, exposure and colour),
before anything is changed to pay for it. No code was changed for this
document; the instrumentation lived in a scratch copy of the tree (see
Method).

The headline: at the three poses profiled, the render thread is the bound,
as the ledger says, and almost none of its cost is drawing. It is per-entity
bookkeeping over tens of thousands of foliage entities that are not on
screen, per-batch-set GPU preprocessing for a few hundred batch sets, the
shadow cascades' casters, and mesh uploads that keep running long after a
static view has "loaded". Upscaling does not touch any of that.

## Method

- Release build of `HEAD` (`50b3be8`) from a `git archive` in the session
  scratchpad, with the HEAD assets (the working tree carried other builders'
  uncommitted shader edits, which a HEAD binary refuses at the city's water),
  and one extra module that is not in the repository. Every two seconds it
  logs every diagnostic in `DiagnosticsStore` (all of Bevy's per-pass GPU
  timers, their CPU encode times and the pipeline statistics: vertex,
  primitive and fragment invocations per pass), the render phases' batch
  sets per view, the mesh entities (total, visible, per shadow cascade),
  the foliage entities by LOD, and the `AssetEvent<Mesh>` traffic. It also
  brackets the sun's cascades with a GPU timer of its own (`sun_shadows`),
  the one large pass Bevy 0.19.1 leaves unscoped: `view_shadow_pass` in
  `bevy_pbr::render::light` opens its render passes with no diagnostic span.
- A second build of the same tree with `--features trace` (Bevy's
  `trace_chrome`) for the per-system CPU tables. `tools/trace_summary.py`
  sums every span over every thread but cannot tell the worlds apart: under
  pipelined rendering the main thread, the render thread and the pool all
  run tasks from both worlds, so the thread a span ran on says nothing. The
  scratch classifier assigns each span its world by the system's name
  (Bevy's `bevy_render`, `bevy_core_pipeline`, `bevy_post_process`,
  `bevy_pbr::render` and the `prepare_`, `queue_`, `write_` and `extract_`
  systems are the render world's; visibility, transforms, physics, streaming
  and material change detection the main world's; each `par_for_each` by its
  query). Times are CPU time summed over threads per frame, under the
  tracer's own overhead (the traced render p50 is 16.5 ms against 11.4 ms
  untraced at the woodland), so they rank costs; they do not add up to the
  frame.
- Poses, one game at a time under `xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 2560x1440x24"`
  at 2560x1440, with the bench's pinned settings (fov 45, foliage distance
  100, `--render-look baseline --visual-time 0.45 --wind 0 --stats
  --bench-warmup 10`):
  - woodland: the bench's `woodland` fixture (`--study-scene woodland
    --flycam --lock-camera --camera-altitude 1.8 --camera-pitch=-0.05
    --look=-1.25`), 1500 frames;
  - city-street: `--at city --look 3.14159`, 1500 frames;
  - flight: `--flight --flight-heading 1.5708 --spawn 15000,-12600
    --flight-secs 30` (30 s, so 20 s measured after the warm-up).

  Each pose ran twice, with the valley fog (the default, the frame with
  phases 1 to 4 on) and with `--fog 0` (the ledger's condition), and once
  traced (700 frames, the last 25 s summarised). One more run at the
  `dusk-street` look fixture (sun on screen) measured the light rays, which
  none of the three poses draws. GPU numbers are the medians of the
  two-second readings after 30 s (15 s for the flight).
- Conditions: other builders were rendering and compiling on the same
  machine. Each run waited until no other game or Xvfb was up and fewer than
  two `rustc` were running, but a run that had started could be overlapped.
  The work percentiles came out higher than the ledger's (render p95 19 to
  27 ms here against 15.8 to 16.4 in Phase 4) and the GPU clock was as
  variable as Phase 2 to 4 recorded. Read the absolute numbers as this
  afternoon's; the proportions within a run are what the tables are for.

One finding changes how the ledger's static fixtures read: they are not
static. The foliage streamer places about 2 400 cells around a new camera
and takes about 50 s to finish (the far trees reach 1.6 km), while the
bench measures from 10 s. Until it finishes, about 150 to 200 new `Mesh`
assets are added every second (one impostor batch per far cell and
species), and the render thread's work spot readings sit at 15 to 24 ms;
after it finishes they sit at 9 to 13 ms at the woodland and 8 to 14 ms at
the city. So a third of every static fixture's measured stretch is
streaming, and its p95 is mostly that third. The steady frame is still
over the 8.3 ms gate on the render thread, by 1 to 4 ms.

## 3. Draws and entities

Bevy 0.19.1 draws every binned phase with GPU culling and multi-draw
indirect, so the CPU issues one draw per batch set and the GPU expands it
to one indirect draw per mesh in the set. Batch sets are therefore the
draw-call count that costs the CPU, and the per-set work (one bind group
built and one compute dispatch for bin unpacking per set, per view, per
frame) is where it shows. The number of distinct meshes per set is not
public in 0.19.1.

## 4. The five largest costs

### 4.1 Foliage entities nobody sees (render and main threads)

Diagnosis. Every placed plant within the mesh levels' 385 m is spawned at all three of its
mesh levels at once, as bark and leaf entities each, and `VisibilityRange`
picks the one that shows; every far cell adds one impostor batch per
species. At the woodland that is 42 740 tree mesh entities and 4 932
impostor batches for 1 503 and 1 007 visible. Bevy pays per entity, not
per visible entity, in `check_visibility` (1.64 ms), `check_visibility_ranges`
(0.97), the material change scans (0.86), the wireframe check (0.67),
`calculate_bounds` (0.47) and `reset_view_visibility` (0.11) on the main
side, about 4.7 ms of thread-summed CPU, and in `extract_visibility_ranges`
(0.56) and the mesh extraction (0.50) during extraction, which the main
thread waits on. The city pays the same per entity on 15 716 tree meshes
and 3 084 batches for 447 and 78 visible.

Candidate fix. Keep one level per plant alive: the streamer already knows
each cell's distance band, so a cell should hold the entities of the level
its band draws and swap them when the band changes (with the crossfade done
by spawning the next level a band early, as now, but despawning the one it
replaces once the fade ends), instead of three resident levels per tree. And
merge a far cell's impostor batches into one entity (one mesh, the species
told apart by an atlas index in the instance record), or batch far trees per
256 m cell. Expected: woodland foliage entities from about 52 000 to about
12 000, main-thread CPU down 2.5 to 3.5 ms summed (1 to 2 ms of the main
p50), extraction down about 0.8 ms of the render frame's critical path.
Dropping `WireframePlugin` from builds that do not ask for F4 is a
separate, one-line 0.5 to 0.7 ms of main-thread CPU.

### 4.2 Batch sets and the per-set GPU preprocessing (render thread and GPU)

Diagnosis. The woodland draws 487 batch sets a frame (98 opaque and 42
alpha mask, the same again in the prepass, 207 over the four cascades), the
city 419, the flight about 150. Bevy 0.19.1 builds a bin unpacking bind
group per set per view per frame (`prepare_preprocess_bind_groups`, 1.23
ms at the woodland, 1.12 at the city, 0.59 on the flight) and dispatches
one bin unpacking compute per set (`bin_unpacking` on the GPU, 0.74, 0.66,
0.27 ms: about 1.5 to 1.8 us per set, for 33 000 invocations of actual work
at the woodland). The sets come from material instances, since a set is
one pipeline and one material bind group: one `GrassMaterial` per grass
cell (`spawn_grass` in `strata-foliage`), one tree material per species and variant, one impostor material
per species and the buildings' materials. The 50 visible grass cells are
likely about half of the woodland's 98 opaque sets and as many of the
prepass's (the flight's 39 visible cells against 51 to 57 sets, the city's 8
against 68, fit the same reading).

Candidate fix. One grass material for all cells, the cell's blades found
through `MeshTag` (already used by the trees) indexing a shared storage
buffer of cell records, so grass collapses from one set per cell to one;
then tree and impostor textures in texture arrays per material kind so a
kind is one set. Expected: woodland sets from 487 to about 250, which by
the linear fit above is about 0.35 ms of GPU and 0.6 ms of render-thread
CPU, and smaller `write_*_instance_buffers` work.

### 4.3 The sun's cascades (GPU and both threads)

Diagnosis. Four cascades from 0.5 m to 20 km at 4096 (`SkySettings`:
`shadow_distance` 20 000, `first_cascade_far_bound` 120). The bounds are
geometric, so the far two cover 660 m to 20 km, where every building,
terrain region and near-level tree casts. At the city that is 1 792, 6 820,
8 903 and 9 134 caster instances (26 649 caster draws per frame), and the
shadow pass is the largest GPU item at that pose (1.69 to 1.77 ms, more
than the opaque pass); the CPU follows: `write_binned_instance_buffers<Shadow>`
1.19 ms and `batch_and_prepare<Shadow>` 0.26 on the render side,
`check_dir_light_mesh_visibility` 1.17 + 0.46 ms on the main side. At the
woodland only near-level trees cast (ADR 0005), so it is 1.45 to 1.52 ms
of GPU for 1 492 caster instances: there the cost is the terrain and the
4096 maps, not the count.

Candidate fix. Bring `shadow_distance` to 3 to 4 km, which keeps the
mountains' shadows that a low sun shows in the valley (beyond that the
aerial perspective has taken most of the contrast) and lets the cascades
tighten (bounds 120, 380, 1 200, 4 000 m); make the far building levels and
the skyline `NotShadowCaster` past the second cascade's reach, as the far
trees already are; and try 3 cascades with the temporal filter. Both are
tunables of `SkySettings` already (`shadow_distance`,
`first_cascade_far_bound`, `shadow_map_size`), so the trial is the F6
panel. Expected at the city: 0.7 to 1.0 ms of GPU and 1.5 to 2 ms of
thread-summed CPU; at the woodland 0.3 to 0.5 ms of GPU.

### 4.4 Mesh uploads that do not stop (render thread)

Diagnosis. `allocate_and_free_meshes` is 0.92 ms a frame at the woodland,
2.69 at the city and 7.74 on the flight, with `update_sparse_buffers` 0.53
to 0.91 and, on the flight, `prepare_erased_assets<GrassCell>` 0.49 (a new
grass material per cell). Counting `AssetEvent<Mesh>` shows what feeds it at
the static poses: 150 to 240 new meshes a second, almost all impostor
batches, each a unique mesh built by `impostor::batch_mesh` for one cell's
trees of one species, for the 50 s the far ring takes to fill. On the
flight it is that plus the terrain regions. This is the render thread's
largest single system on any moving pose, and the reason a static
fixture's p95 is 7 to 10 ms above its p50.

Candidate fix. Draw far trees the way grass is drawn: one shared impostor
quad mesh and a per-cell storage buffer of tree records read in the vertex
shader, so a far cell costs a buffer write and no `Mesh` asset, no slab
allocation and no bin of its own. Short of that, raise
`MeshAllocatorSettings`' slab sizes so the small batch meshes share slabs,
and budget impostor installation per frame the way tree spawns already are.
Separately, the bench's 10 s warm-up should become "until the foliage
streamer is idle" (its `placed` count stops and `in_flight` is 0), or the
static fixtures go on measuring streaming. Expected: 1 to 2.5 ms off the
render thread during fill at the static poses, 4 to 6 ms on the flight.

### 4.5 The opaque geometry, drawn twice (GPU)

Diagnosis. The prepass (depth, normals for SSAO, motion vectors for TAA)
and the opaque pass each process the whole visible geometry: 3.71 M
vertices and 2.20 M primitives apiece at the woodland. The prepass then
runs 14.5 M fragments (3.9 per pixel) because leaf cards and blades are
alpha-masked and discard, which turns early depth off, and the opaque pass
still runs 9.4 M (2.5 per pixel) because masked foliage in the main pass
cannot rely on the prepass's depth either. Together 2.4 ms at the woodland
and 1.3 on the flight. The terrain's four weight attributes are not the
lever here: they are 16 of about 110 bytes a vertex, and the passes are
fragment- and overdraw-bound (the city, with 2.7 M vertices but 0.8
fragments per pixel, spends 0.7 ms on its opaque pass against the
woodland's 1.8). An estimate of slimming them is under 0.1 ms.

Candidate fix. Reduce the masked overdraw near the camera: fewer, larger
leaf cards on the near level, the near band from 66 m to about 45 m (the
middle level takes over earlier), and alpha-to-coverage-style opaque leaf
silhouettes where the mask is mostly solid. Expected 0.3 to 0.6 ms of GPU
at the woodland. This is a GPU saving, so it only matters once 4.1 to 4.4
have brought the render thread under the GPU.

### Also worth a line

- The unscoped GPU (1.0 to 2.3 ms) is mostly the atmosphere, and part of it
  is the `AtmosphereEnvironmentMapLight` regenerated and filtered every
  frame at Bevy's default 512 by 512 per face (plus 0.41 ms of
  `prepare_generated_environment_map_bind_groups` on the render thread).
  A 128 size, or regenerating only when the sun has moved by a degree,
  should give back several tenths of a millisecond on both.
- `prepare_clusters_for_gpu_clustering` costs 0.74 to 0.79 ms of render CPU
  at every pose whatever the light count; worth a look at what it walks
  (the probes, the decals) before Phase 6 adds more clusterables.
- The light rays (0.22 ms with the sun on screen) and the whole Phase 4
  lens (about 0.2 ms) are not where the frame goes.

## 5. Upscaling

What it would buy. Upscaling saves only the passes whose cost scales with
pixels: the fragment part of the opaque pass and the prepass, SSAO, the two
fogs, TAA, bloom, auto exposure and the post stack. At the woodland those
are about 3.4 ms of the 8.2; at the city 1.8 of 6.5; on the flight 2.2 of
4.7. The shadows, bin unpacking, clustering, mesh preprocessing and most of
the atmosphere do not scale. And none of it is the bound: the render
thread, at 11 to 14 ms p50 here and 9 to 13 ms in a settled view, does not
get cheaper with fewer pixels.

DLSS in Bevy 0.19.1 (from the registry):

- The `dlss` feature on `bevy` turns on `bevy_anti_alias/dlss`, which pulls
  in the `dlss_wgpu` crate (not in our registry; it needs fetching and its
  own setup, which links NVIDIA's DLSS SDK under NVIDIA's licence, see
  `bevy_anti_alias/src/dlss/mod.rs`) and `bevy_render/raw_vulkan_init`.
  `DefaultPlugins` then registers `DlssInitPlugin`, which panics unless a
  `DlssProjectId` resource is inserted before `DefaultPlugins`.
- RTX hardware on the Vulkan backend only, on Windows and Linux; the game
  checks `Option<Res<DlssSuperResolutionSupported>>` at runtime and falls
  back.
- The camera gets `Dlss` (its `#[require]`s add `TemporalJitter`,
  `MipBias`, `DepthPrepass`, `MotionVectorPrepass` and `Hdr`) in place of
  `TemporalAntiAliasing`, and DLSS runs in `Core3dSystems::EarlyPostProcess`,
  so bloom, auto exposure, the effect stack and tonemapping stay at full
  resolution and save nothing.
- It sets `MainPassResolutionOverride` on the camera, and everything drawn
  before it must render into `View::main_pass_viewport`. In 0.19.1 only the
  prepass, the opaque, transparent, deferred, transmission, OIT and
  atmosphere nodes read the override; SSAO does not, and neither does Bevy's
  volumetric fog (our local fog volumes) or our own valley fog pass, which
  all run before DLSS. Ours would need porting, and SSAO and the volumetric
  fog a Bevy patch or a check that they are correct as they stand.
- It would sit behind a cargo feature the server and CI never build, which
  `just server-check` and the layering rule already allow.

A render scale of 0.8 with TAA and CAS in Bevy 0.19.1 (from the registry):

- Bevy has no render-scale setting, and `MainPassResolutionOverride` is not
  one: its doc says it does not affect post processing, so TAA and the post
  stack would still run, and read, at the full size. The working route is a
  second camera: the 3D camera renders to an `Image` (`RenderTarget::Image`,
  a component in 0.19.1) at 2048x1152 with everything it has today (TAA,
  SSAO, the fogs, bloom, tonemapping; they all take the view's own size, so
  none of our passes change), and a window camera at a later `order` with
  `Tonemapping::None` draws that image over the full screen and carries
  `ContrastAdaptiveSharpening` (in `bevy_anti_alias`, valid on a 2D or 3D
  camera, default strength 0.6) and the UI (`UiTargetCamera` to the window
  camera). Screen-space picking and the cursor ray scale by the ratio.
- Cost: one 2560x1440 blit and the CAS pass, about the upscaling and
  effect-stack passes above (0.02 to 0.05 ms each). Saving at 0.8: 36 % of
  the pixel-bound passes, about 1.2 ms at the woodland, 0.65 at the city,
  0.8 on the flight. It works on every GPU and needs no SDK.

Recommendation: neither now. Phase 6's target is the render thread, and the
order is 4.4 (impostors as instance records, the warm-up), 4.1 (one level
per plant, merged far batches, no wireframe plugin), 4.2 (one grass
material) and 4.3 (shadow distance), which together are worth 5 to 8 ms of
thread-summed CPU and should bring the settled render thread under the GPU.
When the GPU is the bound, do the 0.8 render scale first, behind a
`RenderLook` field (`render_scale`, validated to 0.5 to 1.0, on the F6
panel) so the look fixtures can judge the softness against CAS; it is a day
of camera plumbing and touches none of the passes. DLSS is the better
image at lower scales on RTX cards and worth a spike behind its feature
after that, once the SDK's licence is acceptable and SSAO, the volumetric
fog and the valley fog have been made to honour `main_pass_viewport`.

## Reproducing

The scratch instrumentation is a module added to `apps/game` in a copy of
the tree, never committed: it logs `DiagnosticsStore` and the phase,
foliage and mesh-event counts every two seconds, and brackets
`per_view_shadow_pass::<EARLY_SHADOW_PASS>` with a GPU time span between
`early_prepass` and `early_downsample_depth`, the way
`strata_atmosphere::local_fog` brackets Bevy's volumetric fog. Turning the
shadow span and the batch-set and foliage counts into a permanent `--stats`
line would make every later phase's cost a diff in the ledger, as Phase 0
asked for the look; that is a small change in `strata-client::stats` and
`strata-atmosphere::diagnostics` and the obvious first step of Phase 6.
