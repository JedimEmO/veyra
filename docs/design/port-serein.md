# Port Serein: the city

Design, 2026-09-27. Names are working names. It follows
[the setting](../SETTING.md) and the [screenshot studies](../research/veyra-style/README.md),
and replaces the first-slice city, which was ashlar's showcase metropolis
dropped onto a flat platform. That city proved the pipeline and nothing
else. It is a grid of identical blocks with carriageways, parked cars and
neon signs, standing inland on a desert plain. None of that has a reason to
exist on Veyra.

This document starts from why Port Serein is where it is and who built it,
and derives the layout and the geometry from that.

Decided: Port Serein is a **company town** and the settlement's **main hub**.
Pelagic Works owns the port, the starport, the water and the processor. The
processor stands **close to the city**. A **starport** and the **Exchange**, a
market beside it, are the trading and questing hub. **Water** is contested.
The tone is grounded sci-fi: nothing whimsical, but the technology is real.

## 1. Why the city is here

The Serein Basin drains to the sea through one sheltered bay. Its western
shore rises steeply out of the water, about 20 m for every 100 m inland, to
a ridge about 90 m up (seed 42, the bay whose water starts 500 m west of the
world origin). Four things meet there:

1. **Deep, sheltered water.** Ships berth against the steep shore without
   dredging, and the headland breaks the swell.
2. **A broad, level headland** above the bay: the only ground near the
   water flat enough for landing pads.
3. **The sea.** The atmospheric processor needs seawater by the million
   tonnes for its exchange towers and its desalination; the bay supplies it.
4. **The pass inland** into the basin's lowlands, where the colony's farms
   and settlements are.

Pelagic Works chose the bay for all four at once. Port Serein is where
everything arrives on Veyra's southern continent, by ship or by shuttle,
and where the machine that is making the basin habitable stands.

## 2. Who owns it

Pelagic Works holds the concession: the land, the quay, the starport, the
processor and the water. It built most of the city, employs most of its
people, and houses many of them. Everyone else lives here on Pelagic's
terms.

- **The Borough Assembly** represents the residents. It runs the Terraces'
  day-to-day life on land it leases from the company, and argues with
  Pelagic over rent, rations, housing and water.
- **The Continuity Institute** monitors the processor and the basin's
  ecology from a station on the ridge, tolerated by Pelagic because the
  concession requires it.
- **The water:** the processor's desalination plant supplies the city.
  Pelagic meters it, prices it and rations it in dry seasons. The Terraces
  keep their own cisterns against the day the meters close. A great deal
  of the city's politics, and a great many jobs, are about water.

## 3. Four generations

The city is about 160 years old. Each generation built for what it needed
and left the previous one standing. The city's look is the four overlapping.

### The Landing (years 0 to 30)

A survey mission and the first settlers. Shuttles came down on the headland;
the settlement grew around the landing field as prefabricated modules.

- **Built:** ivory and pale composite shells with rounded corners, oval
  windows in thick frames, low domes, flat roofs with equipment on them.
  Small, light, shipped.
- **Survives as:** the oldest quarter by the starport. The original survey
  mast, rebuilt twice, is the **beacon tower**, the city's oldest landmark,
  where the Borough Assembly meets. Its modules are now shops, fixers'
  offices and canteens around the Exchange.

### The Concession (years 30 to 120)

Pelagic won the concession and built the city as a company works: quay,
breakwater, cranes, warehouses, the processor on the ridge, the pipelines
between them, the starport's pads, and worker housing by the thousand.

- **Built:** dark cast concrete. Massive retaining walls, slab blocks on
  piers, deep recessed entrances, narrow windows, enclosed bridges between
  buildings, pipe galleries. Built to last and to be controlled: one way
  in, past a gate.
- **Survives as:** most of the city. The Quay, the starport, the processor
  and its pipelines, Pelagic's headquarters, and the housing slabs
  stepping up the hillside.

### The Terraces (years 90 to 150)

Migration outgrew company housing. Settlers leased hillside plots from
Pelagic, cut them into terraces and built on each level themselves.

- **Built:** composite modules stacked two or three high on concrete
  terrace walls, extended with sheds, balconies, rooftop gardens and
  awnings; cisterns and tanks on every few terraces. Painted where the
  owner could afford paint.
- **Survives as:** the **Terraces**, climbing the hillside between the
  company's slabs. This is where people live, trade and meet outside work.

### Now (the last ten years)

The starport has doubled its traffic. Pelagic built a new terminal and is
clearing upper terraces for another housing slab. The Assembly is resisting.

- **Built:** the terminal in pale composite and glass, container housing
  stacked on the upper terraces, scaffolding.
- **Tension made visible:** a half-built Pelagic slab on the upper
  terraces, next to homes it would replace.

## 4. Who lives here and how they move

About 60 000 people, of whom the slice shows the part a player walks
through: dock and pad crews, processor technicians, Pelagic staff, traders,
fabricators, medics, and a steady stream of new arrivals looking for work
and a room.

**Almost nobody owns a vehicle.** People walk, take the transit line, or
ride freight.

- **Walking** is how the city works. Terraces are joined by stairs and
  ramps; lanes run along each terrace, following the contour.
- **The transit line** runs on piers along the hillside, level, from the
  starport terminal past the Exchange to a Quay station, and on up to the
  processor and inland through the pass.
- **Freight** moves by skimmer and crawler on one service road: from the
  starport's cargo apron along the Quay, up through the company yards and
  out through the pass. It never enters the Terraces; past the road,
  goods are carried or lifted.
- **Shuttles** land and lift from the starport pads, day and night: the
  city's sound and its constant motion overhead.

So the streets are not roads. A lane is 3 to 6 m wide, for people and
handcarts. The only vehicles are freight, on their road and aprons.

## 5. Districts

From the water up:

| District | Level | Character | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Quay** | 3 to 8 m | Dark concrete quay wall, crane line, warehouses, the service road, gated berths | Dock crews, freight |
| **The Starport** | 35 to 40 m, on the headland | Landing pads in cradles, the terminal, a control tower, fuel and cryo tanks, the cargo apron | Crews, arrivals |
| **The Exchange** | 35 to 45 m, beside the starport | The market square: stalls under awnings, fixers' offices, job boards, canteens, the beacon tower, the oldest modules | Everyone: the hub |
| **Pelagic Heights** | 25 to 60 m | Headquarters on its own terrace, the housing slabs on piers, enclosed bridges, gates | Pelagic staff and workers |
| **The Terraces** | 25 to 85 m | Leased housing, workshops, cisterns, gardens, container stacks, the half-built slab | Residents |
| **The Processor** | 90 to 200 m, on the ridge above the city | The exchange towers and intake hall, the desalination plant, the pipelines down to the bay, service buildings | Technicians, the Institute |
| **The Pass gate** | about 90 m, beside the processor | Where the road and transit leave inland: a freight depot and checkpoint | Freight crews |

The **Institute station** sits on the ridge beside the processor, where its
instruments can watch both the machine and the basin.

## 6. The hub

The Exchange is where a player spends time between journeys, so it gets the
most care.

- **Arrival.** A new arrival steps off a shuttle, walks through the
  terminal and comes out onto the Exchange. The city's first view is the
  square: stalls, people, the beacon tower, and the processor's towers
  above the terraces.
- **What is there:** traders under awnings and in the old modules; fixers'
  and company offices with job boards; a Borough water office; a canteen or
  two; a clinic; an Institute kiosk. Services a player needs, each with an
  entrance and someone behind a counter.
- **What surrounds it:** the starport on one side, the Terraces climbing
  behind, a stair and the transit station down to the Quay, and a view
  over the bay.

## 7. Architecture by generation

Every building belongs to a generation, and repairs to a building belong to
whoever made them. A composite module patched with a concrete panel and a
Borough-painted door is normal.

| | Landing composites | Pelagic concrete | Terrace vernacular | New |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Share of the city** | Small, old core | Most of it | The hillside between | A few buildings |
| **Mass** | Modules, 1 to 2 storeys, rounded corners | Slabs and blocks, 4 to 14 storeys, on piers or cut into the slope; the processor | Modules stacked 2 to 4 high on terrace walls, many additions | Larger, lighter |
| **Materials** | Ivory composite, pale grey panels | Charcoal cast concrete, dark steel, pipe | Composite, concrete, corrugated sheet, fabric | Pale composite, glass, white steel |
| **Openings** | Oval and rounded windows in thick frames | Slit windows in deep reveals, recessed entrances | Doors onto balconies and lanes, shutters | Wide glazing |
| **Roofs** | Flat with equipment, low domes | Flat, parapets, plant rooms, pipe racks | Terraces and gardens, tanks, awnings | Canopies |
| **Colour** | Ivory, muted teal | Grey to near black; sparse orange safety marks | Faded paint: green, oxide red, ochre, cream | White, Institute teal |
| **Wear** | Yellowed, patched | Salt stains, runoff streaks, old stencils | Everything, and personal care | Barely any |

Across all four: **structure is visible.** Retaining walls, piers, pipes,
stairs, cranes, pads and the processor are the city's big shapes.

## 8. Signs, light and sound

Signs follow who put them up.

- **Pelagic** stencils block letters on concrete: the company name, berth,
  pad and gate numbers. Sparse, big, faded. Cold white work lights on arms,
  pad lights on the starport.
- **The Borough** paints wayfinding by hand on terrace walls and stair
  landings: terrace names, arrows to the Exchange, the clinic, the water
  office.
- **The Exchange** hangs painted boards and fabric awnings; a few stalls
  have one small illuminated sign; canteens have strings of warm lamps.
- **Lit wayfinding** belongs to the terminal, the transit stations and the
  pads. There are no holograms and no advertising screens.

At night the Terraces and the Exchange glow warm; the Quay and Pelagic
Heights are cold white pools with dark between; the pads ring with landing
lights; the processor's towers carry red warning lights and steam in its
floodlights; the transit piers carry a line of lamps along the hillside.

## 9. Layout

1. **Contours first.** Terraces follow the hillside's contours, about 6 m
   apart in height and 12 to 25 m deep. Their walls are the city's
   defining lines seen from the bay.
2. **The headland** holds the starport and the Exchange side by side,
   facing the bay: the city's centre.
3. **The processor on the ridge** above the city is its skyline and its
   landmark from everywhere in the basin. Its pipelines run straight down
   the hillside to the bay intake, in a cut through the Terraces, and are
   the city's second spine.
4. **The main stair** climbs from the Quay to the Exchange; a second stair
   runs beside the pipelines from the Exchange up to the processor.
5. **The transit line** runs level along the hillside at about 30 m, on
   piers, from the terminal past the Exchange to a Quay station, then up to
   the processor and the pass.
6. **The service road** runs from the starport's cargo apron down to the
   Quay, then climbs through the company yards in switchbacks to the pass.
   It is the only road.
7. **Pelagic Heights** stands between the Quay and the Terraces: the
   headquarters on its own terrace with a gate, and the slabs on piers
   stepping up the hill.

The road from the wilderness arrives at the pass gate beside the processor.
The first view from the road is the city falling away to the bay below the
towers, with shuttles over the headland.

## 10. What the world must do

Settlement support today (ADR 0009) flattens a disc. Port Serein needs more:

- **Site choice:** a coast facing sheltered water, with a hillside of 15 to
  25% grade over at least 300 m inland, a headland, and a ridge above.
  The bay west of the origin on seed 42 fits. Site scoring gains coast,
  grade and aspect terms and loses the preference for flat ground.
- **Terracing:** `sample()` shapes the hillside into level terraces between
  given contour heights, sharply stepped between them. The retaining walls
  are geometry, so the terrain only needs to be level on each terrace and
  steep between them. The headland, the Quay and the processor's platform
  are more levels.
- **Cuts:** the pipeline cut and the service road bench are graded strips.
- **The Quay edge:** the terrain meets the water at a vertical quay wall,
  which is geometry.
- **Placement** keeps clear of all of it, as today. Planters and courtyard
  trees are placed by the plan, not by the forest.

## 11. The slice's share

The whole city is too much for the next step. The slice needs the part a
player meets on arrival and on the first job:

1. **The starport:** two pads, the terminal, the control tower, tanks.
2. **The Exchange** with its stalls, offices, beacon tower and canteen.
3. **The main stair** down to a stretch of **the Quay** with the crane
   line and one warehouse.
4. **Two terraces** beside the stair, and the face of one **Pelagic slab**.
5. **The processor** on the ridge, at full scale in silhouette, with its
   pipelines down the hill, even if only its base is walkable.
6. **One transit station** and a few hundred metres of piers.
7. **The pass gate**, where the road from the wilderness arrives.

The rest of the Terraces, Pelagic's headquarters, the Institute station and
the half-built slab follow once these hold up in the engine.
